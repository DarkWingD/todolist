Why it's laid out like this

Left is now. Where everyone is, what's on, dinner, what's due. It's the Today screen with the chrome removed and the type tripled.

Centre is the week. Seven columns, always Monday to Sunday, today filled grey, grown-ups' days off hatched. Kids' events are dashed so a child can find their own at a glance.

Right is the kids. Their next three weeks (carnival, pupil-free day, holidays) and their jobs with real checkboxes, tall enough that a seven-year-old can read them.

E-ink rules the design follows

No colour, no thin lines. Everything is 2px or heavier, and meaning is carried by weight, dashes and hatching rather than hue.

Big type. Body text is 24–27px on an 1872px panel, about 4 mm tall on a 10-inch screen: readable from two metres.

Static by design. Nothing animates, nothing scrolls. The page is a snapshot the panel refreshes every half hour; the footer says when.

Ticking a chore would happen on a phone; the wall just shows it done at the next refresh. A touch panel could tick in place later.

How it would be built

A /wall route in the web app that renders this page from the same API (Today, calendar range, kids' events, kids' tasks), with a device token instead of a sign-in. The panel's controller fetches it as a screenshot on a timer; most e-ink frames (Inkplate, Kindle mods, Visionect) do exactly this.

The 1872×1404 size is the common 10.3" panel. The layout is a grid, so a 7.5" or 13.3" panel is a scale change, not a redesign.
